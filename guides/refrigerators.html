Refrigerator buying guide

The refrigerator is the hardest-working appliance in your home — it runs 24/7 for a decade or more. Here's how to pick the right type, check that it works, and make sure it actually fits through your front door.

The four types

French door — double doors up top, freezer drawer below. The most popular style: wide shelves fit platters and pizza boxes, and you only open half the fridge at a time.

Side-by-side — fridge on one side, freezer on the other. Narrow doors are great for tight kitchens, but wide items don't fit.

Top-freezer — the classic. The most refrigerator for the money and the most reliable layout ever made.

Bottom-freezer — fresh food at eye level, freezer in a pull-out drawer below. A nice middle ground.

What to check before you buy

  • It should be genuinely cold — fridge around 37°F, freezer at 0°F. Every unit here is plugged in and tested before listing.
  • If it has an ice maker or water dispenser, run both.
  • Check the door seals: close the door on a dollar bill — if it slides out easily, the seal is weak.
  • Look for excessive frost in the freezer, which can signal a seal or defrost issue.

Measuring your space

This is where most refrigerator purchases go wrong. Measure width, height (including the hinge!), and depth of the opening. Then measure your doorways and hallways — a 36-inch-wide fridge does not fit through a 32-inch door, and the doors usually come off to buy you a couple of inches. Decide between counter-depth (flush with cabinets, less capacity) and standard depth (sticks out a few inches, holds more).

This is where scratch-and-dent wins biggest

Refrigerators are the category where the scratch-and-dent math is most dramatic. The jump from a basic top-freezer to a French door changes your daily life — wide shelves, ice and water in the door, a freezer drawer that actually organizes. And a dented French door routinely costs the same as a pristine basic model. Buy the nicer fridge. The dent lives on the side; the convenience lives in your kitchen.

Features: worth it or not?

Ice and water in the door are genuinely convenient — and the most common thing to need a repair down the road. Door-in-door and smart screens are nice but add cost and complexity. For pure reliability-per-dollar, a basic top-freezer is almost impossible to beat.
